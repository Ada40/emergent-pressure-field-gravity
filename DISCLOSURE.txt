Abstract
We present a novel framework in which gravitational phenomena emerge from spatial gradients in a vacuum-like pressure field, rather than from a fundamental force or hypothetical dark matter particles. This pressure-field model reproduces observed rotation curves of the Milky Way (MW) and Andromeda (M31) galaxies using only their baryonic mass distributions, without parameter tuning or curve-fitting.
This disclosure provides both a timestamped record of the discovery and a concrete, reproducible computational framework for independent verification.
1. Introduction
The anomalous flattening of galactic rotation curves has traditionally been attributed to dark matter. Alternative approaches, such as Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) and entropic or emergent gravity, provide conceptual explanations but often lack concrete, testable mechanisms.
Here, we present an emergent gravity framework in which gravitational acceleration arises as a consequence of a spatially structured pressure field induced by baryonic mass. This framework:
Is mathematically well-defined and reproducible.
Accounts for rotation curves of MW and M31 without dark matter.
Offers a testable model that can be extended to other galaxies.

Gravitational phenomena appear to emerge naturally from a vacuum pressure field influenced by baryonic mass.
